        What you just saw

        Unlearning runs training backwards on the one fact until the model stops saying it. It works, if the only test is asking the question. But the weights do not go back to where they were. They go somewhere new, and every old answer shifts a little with them.

        Restoring from the record puts back a saved copy of the weights. It is exact, and it is only possible if somebody kept the copy before the change was made.

        Why it matters

        If your information ends up inside a model, "we removed it" can mean either of these. One is a model that no longer says it. The other is a model that is provably the one that never learned it. I expect the law to start asking which.

        My research, retractbench, asks the same question of real language models and published undo methods.

        What this is not

        This is a toy model: 1,560 weights, not billions, and it does not read your name. It gives you a row of numbers of your own, the same way it holds the ten people it was trained on. The unlearning here is the simplest kind there is. Better methods exist and leave less behind. The gap between "no longer says it" and "exactly as before" is what stays.

        The network and its training are in one script, written out by hand with no libraries.
