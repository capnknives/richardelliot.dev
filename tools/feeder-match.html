      Before and after the meal

      Thaw frozen feeders in the fridge, or in a sealed bag in cool water. Then warm them in warm water to about body temperature, 98 to 100°F. Never microwave one, and never refreeze one.

      Leave the snake alone for 48 hours after it eats. Handling too soon is a common reason a meal comes back up.

      If a meal does come back up, wait 14 days before feeding again, then offer one size smaller. Twice in a month means a visit to a reptile vet.

      How it is worked out

      Every snake is an individual, so treat the ticket as a starting point and compare the feeder to the snake. It should be about as wide as the snake at its widest point. For a hognose, no wider than the head.

      For corn snakes and kingsnakes the meal is a share of body weight that tapers as the snake grows: about 15% for a hatchling, 10% for a juvenile, and 6% for an adult. A flat 10% for life is the common rule, and it overfeeds adults. Adults are also held to a largest mouse size, because past that point a bigger meal mostly adds fat.

      Hognoses use the weight ladder that two breeders publish (Ectotherm Empire and San Diego Reptile) instead of a percentage. Corn snake schedules follow ReptiFiles. Nobody publishes a veterinary chart for Florida kingsnakes, so those numbers are general kingsnake care and keeper practice, and the ticket says so.

      The feeder names and gram ranges are a placeholder: one large supplier's chart. Suppliers do not agree on what a "small" is, so the match is done in grams first and named second.

      The arithmetic is in one short script you can read.

      Why it is here

      A friend breeds snakes and the mice and rats they eat. Every buyer asks him the same question: what size, and how many. This is that answer as a tool, and the next version runs on his own size chart and prices.
