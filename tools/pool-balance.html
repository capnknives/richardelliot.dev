    Before you dose

    These are estimates. Add two thirds of any dose, circulate, retest, then add the rest if the water still needs it. Add acid to water, never water to acid, and never mix pool chemicals with each other.

    How it is worked out

    The saturation index is the Langelier index in the form the pool trade uses: pH, plus factors for temperature, calcium hardness and carbonate alkalinity, minus a constant that rises with dissolved solids. It is the same sum as the chart in a service manual, with the factors worked out instead of read from rounded rows, so it can differ from a hand calculation by a few hundredths. Between −0.3 and +0.3 counts as balanced.

    Hardly anyone carries a dissolved solids meter, so the pool type stands in for that reading: about 1,000 ppm for a chlorine pool and about 3,200 for a salt pool. If you do have a reading, there is a place to enter it.

    Stabilizer shows up in an alkalinity test, so the part that belongs to cyanurate is taken out first. Acid and soda ash doses come from the carbonate and cyanurate equilibria instead of a lookup chart, so they account for how well your water is buffered. Baking soda, calcium chloride and stabilizer doses are plain stoichiometry.

    The arithmetic is in one short script you can read.
